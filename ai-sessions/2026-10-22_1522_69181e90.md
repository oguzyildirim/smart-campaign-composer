**How delivery works in the M5 draft:**
1. **Approval queues everything in one step.** The approval transaction also creates one delivery row per consented recipient and channel, plus "outbox" rows (a table of messages waiting to be published). So either everything is saved or nothing is. A database rule makes duplicate deliveries impossible, so approving twice creates nothing new.
2. **Getting jobs into Kafka.** The worker publishes outbox rows to Kafka, with one topic per channel (`deliveries.sms`, `deliveries.email`). A job can occasionally be published twice; the next step makes that harmless.
3. **Sending one delivery:**
   - The worker first marks the delivery as "processing" with a time limit, which only works once per delivery, so duplicate jobs are skipped.
   - It re-checks consent and re-checks the personalized text against the length limits.
   - It waits for a rate-limit slot, then calls the mock provider with the delivery ID as the idempotency key.
   - The result is recorded as sent, retrying or failed.
4. **Retries:**
   - Network errors, timeouts, 408, 429 and 5xx are retried; other 4xx errors are permanent.
   - The wait between attempts grows from 1 second to a 60-second cap, with random jitter, for at most 5 attempts.
   - A provider's `Retry-After` value is respected.
   - Retries are scheduled in Postgres rather than with extra Kafka retry topics, and go through the same rate limiter as first attempts.
5. **Rate limit:** a shared counter per channel in Postgres, using the database clock so workers' clocks don't matter. The steady rate plus the allowed burst is capped at 100, e.g. 95/s plus a burst of 5. That guarantees no more than 100 sends in any one-second window, not just on average.
6. **Crash recovery:** deliveries stuck in "processing" after their time limit are retried. Because the provider deduplicates by delivery ID, a crash right after a successful send can't cause a second delivery.
7. **Mock provider:**
   - Failure modes you can configure: slow responses, 5xx, 429 with `Retry-After`, permanent errors, and "accepted but the response was lost".
   - It enforces its own 100/s limit per channel.
   - It rejects any recipient that isn't fake, as a second safety net.
   - A stats endpoint reports duplicates suppressed and the highest per-second rate it saw.
   - Its state is kept in memory, so a restart forgets which keys it has seen (documented as a limitation).

**Two decisions to confirm:**
- **Rate limiter in Postgres**, not Redis. It keeps the stack smaller but adds some database load at about 100 updates per second per channel. Taking a few slots at a time reduces that.
- **Retries scheduled in Postgres**, not Kafka retry topics. They survive restarts, are easy to inspect, and go through the same rate limiter.
